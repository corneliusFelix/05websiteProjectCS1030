USS Nimitz “Tic Tac,” U.S.

November, 2004.

On November 14 2004, Commander David Fravor, commanding officer of Strike Fighter Squadron 41 aboard the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group, was flying an F/A-18F Super Hornet off the coast of Southern California when he was redirected to investigate radar indications of an unidentified target. According to Fravor, a radar controller aboard the guided-missile cruiser USS Princeton informed him the ship's Aegis Combat System had been tracking unusual radar contacts for about two weeks. These targets reportedly appeared at high altitude, descended rapidly toward the sea, and hovered around 20,000 feet.

Fravor reported seeing a white, oval, solid-bodied object—roughly 40 feet long with no visible wings, engines, or control surfaces—hovering just above a churn of white water in the ocean. Fravor and fellow pilot Lieutenant Commander Alex Dietrich, along with their respective weapons systems officers, observed the object for approximately five minutes. Fravor stated that as he spiraled down to inspect it, the object maneuvered toward his aircraft before accelerating away at high speed.

Shortly after Fravor's flight returned to the carrier, another F/A-18F Super Hornet already airborne on a training flight was vectored to the same area. Weapons systems officer Lieutenant Chad Underwood acquired the target on his jet's Raytheon AN/ASQ-228 Advanced Targeting Forward-Looking Infrared (ATFLIR) pod, recording the iconic infrared video dubbed "FLIR1." Underwood, who coined the nickname "Tic Tac" to describe the object's shape, did not make visual contact with his eyes, focusing instead on maintaining radar and thermal lock for intelligence analysis.

Other Navy personnel aboard the Princeton and Nimitz later corroborated key details. Senior radar and combat-systems technicians confirmed that the strike group's AN/SPY-1B radar had logged multiple unexplained contacts dropping from extreme altitudes during the exercise, while aviation technicians noted that radar data and telemetry files from the events were subsequently secured by intelligence personnel.
